Step 1 — Understand the daily sequence

Every training day follows this exact order
1
Pre-workout mobility
7–13 min depending on the day. Lower days = 13 min full protocol. Upper days = 7 min. Conditioning = 8 min. Never skip this. It is not warming up — it is fixing the structural problems that would stop you training in 6 months.
→ See Mobility tab
2
Main strength session
The core of the plan. 45–60 min. This never changes across the 2-week block — only loads progress. Lower A (squat focus) · Lower B (hinge focus) · Lower C (weak points) · Upper Push · Upper Pull · Conditioning.
→ See Week 1 or Week 2 tab, expand the day
3
Minor add-ons
5–6 min tacked onto the end of specific days. Foot conditioning (Mon/Thu/Sun), Neck (Tue/Fri), Traps (Fri). These are the small fixes that prevent the big injuries over 2 years. Week 1 = intro version. Week 2 = progressed version.
→ See Minor tab for full reference
4
Evening run (select days only)
Only on Tue, Fri, Sat. Minimum 4–6 hours after the morning session. Mon, Thu, Sun are heavy leg days — running on those nights would hurt recovery. The plan is designed around this logic.
→ See Evening Run tab

Step 3 — Understand the 2-week block logic

What changes, what doesn't
Week 1 → Week 2: Changes
• Minor add exercises progress
• Run distances increase
• Intervals get longer reps
• Trap work gets heavier
Always stays the same
• All mobility protocols
• All main strength sessions
• Which days you run
• Wednesday full rest
What progresses BETWEEN block repeats (every 2 weeks)
Add weight to your main lifts each repeat. Add 1 interval rep or +100m to Tuesday runs. Add +0.5 km to Friday easy run. The block itself is the framework — you load it heavier each time you repeat it.
After Sunday of Week 2, go back to Monday of Week 1. This block can repeat for the full 2 years. The strength progresses through load, not exercise changes. The run progresses through distance. The minor components stay the same exercises — they just get harder within each block.

Step 5 — The non-negotiables

Rules you don't bend — for 2 years of staying healthy
🚫
Never run on Mon, Thu, or Sun evenings. These are your three lower-body days. Squats, deadlifts, and hip thrusts fatigue the exact muscles running depends on. One run on a wrecked posterior chain is how you get a pulled hamstring that sets you back 6 weeks.
🚫
Never skip Wednesday rest. Six days of two-a-days demand one full rest. Your CNS, your joints, and your hormones all need this. Moving Wednesday rest to another day defeats the purpose.
🚫
Never skip the tibialis raise before treadmill. Shin splints are the most common reason new runners stop running. The tibialis raise is a 3-minute fix. It is in every combined session and every evening run cooldown — do it.
🚫
Never increase both run distance and pace at the same time. Add distance first — keep pace easy. Once distance is comfortable, then tighten pace. Doing both together is how stress fractures happen.
✅
If you miss a day — just skip it. Don't try to make it up. Don't cram two sessions into one day. Move to the next day in the plan. The block is designed with enough frequency that missing one day changes nothing. Cramming destroys recovery.

Step 6 — How to know it's working

Month by month — what to expect
Month 1 — Blocks 1 & 2
You'll feel clunky switching between gym and running. Legs may feel heavy on Tue evening. That's normal — your body is adapting to two stimuli. Foot conditioning will feel pointless. Do it anyway. You're building the base the whole plan runs on.
Month 2–3 — Blocks 3–6
Tuesday intervals will start to feel manageable. Friday easy run approaches 4–5 km. Gym lifts keep climbing. You'll notice hip flexors are looser. Squats feel more fluid. Arch in your foot starts to hold shape.
Month 4–6 — Blocks 7–12
Friday run hits 5 km. This is when you evolve the plan — add a proper long run day on Saturday (replace the jog). Strength numbers should be clearly above where you started. You're a hybrid athlete now, not a gym person who runs.
Year 2
Same block, heavier loads, longer runs. The strength foundation from year 1 makes every run easier. Your body knows the pattern — you're training instead of adapting. This is where the real gains compound.

Phase map

Base — months 1–2 · adaptation
Long run grows 3 → 4.5 km. Slow on purpose. Tendons and shin bone need 6–8 weeks to remodel — pushing here is the #1 cause of running injury. Pace = conversational only. Shin-rule: any pain > 3/10, stop the run.
Build 1 — months 3–4 · adding load
Long run 4.5 → 6.5 km. Third run becomes a true 3rd run (not just a jog). Introduce one tempo segment on Tuesday. Friday easy stays easy.
Build 2 — month 5 · first milestone
Long run 6.5 → 8 km. 8 km hit. Biggest psychological win — most runners quit before this. Keep Saturday short.
Peak — month 6 · the patience month
Long run 8 → 9.5 km. Highest injury risk window — you feel strong, want to push, body still catching up. Trust the deload weeks.
Goal — month 7 · 10 km
Long run 9.5 → 10 km. Goal hit. After this: maintain at 8 km long run or pick a new goal (sub-60 min 10 km, half-marathon base, etc.).

Running form — hybrid athlete priorities

Cadence over stride lengthPriority 1

Strength athletes default to long strides — high injury risk. Target 170–180 steps/min. Count one foot for 30s × 2. Shorter, quicker steps.

Foot strike — midfootInjury prevention

With 5 years of squatting you have ankle strength. Land midfoot, not heel. Your foot conditioning directly builds this capacity.

Arms — 90°, don't cross midlineEconomy

Drive elbows back, not forward. Your trap and neck work stabilises the upper back here.

Posture — tall, lean from anklesPosture

Chin tucked (neck work trains this). Core braced — not rigid, just switched on. Your deadlift bracing transfers directly.

Nasal breathing = Zone 2 testAerobic base

If you can only mouth-breathe on an easy run, you're going too fast. Slow down. Nasal breathing is the litmus test.

Same-day training management

Gap between sessions4–8 hrs

Minimum 4 hours between morning gym and evening run. Allows glycogen partial resynthesis and CNS recovery.

Post-gym nutrition before runEat after gym

Proper meal — rice, protein, some fat. Don't run fasted post-gym. Wait 90 min after eating before running.

Hydration3–4 L/day

Two sessions = double sweat. At 83 kg in Greater Noida climate, minimum 3–4 L on training days. Electrolytes if heavy sweating.
